Why the Black-Scholes Model is Good and the Gaussian Copula is Not

Dariusz Gątarek

What This Talk Covers

Gątarek's provocation: Black-Scholes is good and the Gaussian copula is not — because one is a hedging technology whose errors self-correct through recalibration, while the other is a static correlation assumption with no feedback mechanism.

Plain English Notes

The Big Picture: BS is wrong but self-correcting: the desk re-hedges daily and recalibrates hourly. The copula was wrong and static: correlation was marked once and left to detonate in 2008. The difference isn't accuracy — it's the feedback loop.

Core Mathematics

Gaussian copula default correlation
$$C(u_1,\dots,u_n) = \Phi_\Sigma\!\left( \Phi^{-1}(u_1), \dots, \Phi^{-1}(u_n) \right)$$
