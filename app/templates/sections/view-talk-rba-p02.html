Random Behaviour of Assets

Part 02 of 2 in this series

Series Overview

The empirical starting point of quantitative finance: examining real return data, the random walk model, and the scaling behaviour that leads to Brownian motion as the continuous-time limit.

Plain English Notes

The Big Picture: Measure returns over shorter and shorter windows and a fingerprint emerges: the drift shrinks linearly, the noise shrinks like a square root. The square root wins at small timescales — which is why randomness, not the trend, dominates day-to-day prices.

Core Mathematics

Simple return
$$R_i = \frac{S_{i+1} - S_i}{S_i} \approx \mu\,\delta t + \sigma\,\phi\sqrt{\delta t},\quad \phi \sim N(0,1)$$
Scaling signature
$$\mathbb{E}[R] \sim \delta t, \qquad \operatorname{sd}(R) \sim \sqrt{\delta t}$$
Continuous-time limit
$$dS = \mu S\,dt + \sigma S\,dW_t$$
