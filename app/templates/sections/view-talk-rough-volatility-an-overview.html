Rough Volatility: An Overview

Volatility & Smile — CQF Talks

What This Talk Covers

Why volatility is 'rough': empirical log-volatility increments scale like fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent H ≈ 0.1, far below the H = 1/2 of standard models. Covers the RFSV model, its microstructural foundation, and the term structure of the ATM implied-volatility skew.

Plain English Notes

The Big Picture: Volatility wiggles far more violently at short timescales than diffusion models allow. Making it 'rough' — fractal, with memory — reproduces two stubborn facts at once: the statistics of realized volatility and the explosive short-dated skew in option markets.

Core Mathematics

Fractional Brownian motion scaling
$$\mathbb{E}\left[ \left| \log\sigma_{t+\Delta} - \log\sigma_t \right|^2 \right] \;\propto\; \Delta^{2H}, \qquad H \approx 0.1$$
RFSV volatility dynamics
$$\sigma_t = \sigma\, \exp\!\left( \nu\, W^H_t \right), \qquad W^H = \text{fBM with Hurst } H$$
ATM skew term structure
$$\psi(\tau) := \left| \frac{\partial \sigma_{BS}}{\partial k} \right|_{k=0} \;\sim\; \tau^{\,H - 1/2}$$
