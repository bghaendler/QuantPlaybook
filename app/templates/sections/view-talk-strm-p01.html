Structural Models (credit)

Part 01 of 4 in this series

Series Overview

Credit risk from the balance sheet up: Merton's insight that equity is a call option on the firm's assets, distance to default, first-passage extensions (Black-Cox), and what structural models say about credit spreads.

Plain English Notes

The Big Picture: A leveraged firm's shareholders hold a call option: if assets end above the debt, they keep the difference; if not, they walk away. Default is the option expiring worthless — so option pricing machinery prices default risk, mapping balance-sheet volatility into credit spreads.

Core Mathematics

Equity as a call on firm assets
$$E_0 = A_0 \Phi(d_1) - D e^{-rT} \Phi(d_2)$$
Distance to default
$$DD = \frac{\ln(A_0/D) + (\mu - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_A^2)T}{\sigma_A \sqrt{T}}$$
Credit spread
$$s(T) = -\frac{1}{T}\ln\frac{P(0,T)}{D\,Z(0,T)} \;>\; 0$$
