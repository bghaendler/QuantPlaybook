Martingales

Part 03 of 4 in this series

Series Overview

The probabilistic backbone of arbitrage-free pricing: conditional expectation, filtrations and information, the martingale property, stopping times and the optional stopping theorem, and why discounted prices must be martingales under the risk-neutral measure.

Plain English Notes

The Big Picture: A martingale is a fair game: given everything you know today, tomorrow's expected value is today's value. No-arbitrage forces discounted asset prices to be fair games under the risk-neutral measure — that single idea generates every pricing formula in the course.

Core Mathematics

Martingale property
$$\mathbb{E}[X_t \,|\, \mathcal{F}_s] = X_s, \qquad s \le t$$
Tower property
$$\mathbb{E}\big[\,\mathbb{E}[X \,|\, \mathcal{F}_t]\, \big|\, \mathcal{F}_s\big] = \mathbb{E}[X \,|\, \mathcal{F}_s], \qquad s \le t$$
Exponential martingale
$$M_t = \exp\!\left(\sigma W_t - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^2 t\right)$$
Risk-neutral pricing
$$V_t = \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\!\left[ e^{-r(T-t)}\, V_T \,\middle|\, \mathcal{F}_t \right]$$
